# ==Triangle Dominance Rule==  
  
In traditional cube-based 4D mathematics, singularities refer to mathematical breakdowns where calculations become undefined or unstable. Here's what I mean:  
  
### ++Cartesian Coordinate Singularities in 4D++  
  
**Projection Singularities**: When projecting from 4D to 3D using standard Cartesian methods, certain points become mathematically undefined - particularly when the W coordinate approaches the perspective focal point, causing division by zero.  
  
**Rotation Matrix Singularities**: Traditional 4D rotation matrices can become singular (non-invertible) at certain angles, causing gimbal lock-like effects where rotational freedom is lost.  
  
**Cross-Section Artifacts**: Square-based cross-sectioning creates mathematical discontinuities where vertices suddenly appear/disappear, rather than smooth transitions  
  
**How Triangular Dominance Eliminates These:**  
The triangular approach resolves these because:  
  
**Barycentric Stability**: Triangular coordinates never hit singularities since they're based on ratios that remain well-defined  
  
**Natural Tessellation**: Triangle subdivision creates smooth, continuous surfaces without the edge cases that plague cube-based methods  
  
**Golden Ratio Mathematics**: Using phi (golden ratio) in the triangular calculations provides inherent mathematical stability  
This is why when you switch between tesseract/hypercube objects in the visualization, you should notice smoother, more stable transformations compared to other 4D objects that still use the traditional Cartesian approach. The triangular method eliminates the visual "popping" or sudden geometry changes that occur in square-based 4D projections.
