Key Eligibility for "Detection Systems"
Independent Analysis: Information is considered "original" if it comes from your independent knowledge or independent evaluation of non-public information. Using a custom-built system to uncover previously unknown patterns of crime qualifies.
Non-Public Data: The information must not be derived solely from public sources (like news reports or public lawsuits) unless your specialized analysis provides new, "material" insights that the government didn't already have.
Individuals Only: Only individuals (or groups of individuals) can claim the reward, not corporations or other legal entities.
Excluded Roles: You are generally ineligible if you learned the information through your job as a compliance officer, internal auditor, or if you were hired by the company specifically to conduct an internal investigation.

Reward Structure
If your detection leads to a successful forfeiture exceeding $1 million, you may be eligible for:
Up to 30% of the first $100 million in net proceeds forfeited.
Up to 5% of any additional net proceeds between $100 million and $500 million.

Critical "First-in-the-Door" Rule
The DOJ operates on a "race to report" basis. To be eligible, you must be the first to voluntarily provide the information to the DOJ before they already know about it or before another whistleblower beats you to it.
